### Open Problem 3 has an explicit answer for the published quasimode

For the particular \(w_N\) in Paper 2, I obtain the Dirichlet-norm limit
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\lim_{\substack{N\to\infty\\N=4m+2}}
 N^{3/2}\frac{\|(T_N-\tfrac12)w_N\|}{\|w_N\|}
 =
 \sqrt{\frac{\pi^4}{30}+\frac{17\pi^2}{12}-\zeta(3)-12}
 =2.006701862804673\ldots .}
\]
The finite-\(N\) upper bound in the theorem remains valid. The following calculation resolves the first question at line 511. [Quasimode and residual form, lines 440–455](/Users/mw/Desktop/math/remath/studies/03-sobolev-wave-attractors/papers/paper2_tilt_rigidity_circle_limit/paper2_tilt_rigidity_circle_limit.tex:440) · [Open Problem 3](/Users/mw/Desktop/math/remath/studies/03-sobolev-wave-attractors/papers/paper2_tilt_rigidity_circle_limit/paper2_tilt_rigidity_circle_limit.tex:511)

1. Pull the polygon back to the disk by \(\Psi_N(r,t)=rR_N(t)e^{it}\). Its derivative differs from the identity by \(O(N^{-1})\) almost everywhere. The pulled-back correction is \(r^Ng_N(t)\), where \(g_N=1-R_N^2\). The paper proves \(\|\nabla E_N\|=O(N^{-3/2})\). Hence replacing the pulled-back Dirichlet metric and residual form by their disk values changes the residual norm by \(O(N^{-5/2})\). Also \(\|w_N\|\to\sqrt{2\pi}\).
2. On a face with normal direction \(\nu\), put \(y=N(t-\nu)\). Then \(N^2g_N(\nu+y/N)\to G(y)=\pi^2-y^2\) in periodic \(H^1(-\pi,\pi)\). Its limiting Fourier coefficients have magnitudes \(2\pi^2/3\) at zero and \(2/k^2\) at \(k\ne0\). The input frequencies are \(n=kN\). The disk residual form \(\frac12\int\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\nabla E\cdot\nabla\bar v\) sends them to the mutually disjoint output frequencies \(n\pm2\).
3. For input \(r^Ne^{int}\), Dirichlet Poisson inversion gives the two terms of the Riesz representative, up to overall sign,
   \[
   \frac{N\mp n}{4(N\pm n+2)}
   (r^N-r^{|n\pm2|})e^{i(n\pm2)t}.
   \]
   The squared Dirichlet norm of \((r^N-r^m)e^{imt}\) is \(\pi(N-m)^2/N\). Applying this identity and dividing by \(\|w_N\|^2\) gives
   \[
   C_*^2=\frac{\pi^4}{36}
   +\frac14\sum_{k\ge1}
      \frac{(k+1)^2+(k-1)^4/(k+1)^2}{k^4}.
   \]
   The normalized mode weights are uniformly \(O((1+|k|)^2)\), so the periodic \(H^1\) convergence justifies passage to the infinite sum, including the endpoint modes \(k=\pm1\). Partial fractions give the boxed value.

The parenthetical optimization at line 511 needs its own quantifiers. If it means the infimum over approximating sequences of their scaled residual limsup, allowing a sequence-dependent constant in the \(O(N^{-3/2})\) closeness condition, **that infimum is zero**. On the disk take a collar of width \(d=M/N\). Cover its boundary by finitely many arcs, each with a characteristic unit direction \(e=(1,\pm1)/\sqrt2\) transverse to the arc’s normal, so \(|e\cdot{\bf t}|>c\). Extend \(g_N\) on each arc by \(f(e\cdot x)\) and apply a partition of unity and collar cutoff. Here \(\|f\|_\infty=O(N^{-2})\), \(\|f'\|_\infty=O(N^{-1})\), and \((\partial_x^2-\partial_z^2)f(e\cdot x)=0\). The correction has \(H^1\) norm \(O(N^{-1}\sqrt d+N^{-2}/\sqrt d)\). Its residual is \(O(N^{-2}/\sqrt d)\) in \(H^{-1}\), by tangential integration by parts on the \(f'\) term and \(\|v\|_{L^2(\mathrm{collar})}\le Cd\|\nabla v\|\) on the cutoff terms. For fixed \(M\) the pullback error is lower order. The scaled limsup is therefore \(O(M^{-1/2})\). Letting \(M\to\infty\) gives the infimum. A prescribed closeness constant would define a different optimization problem.
