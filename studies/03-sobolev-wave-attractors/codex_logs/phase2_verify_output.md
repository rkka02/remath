## Two counterexamples that block certification

**False period exclusion.** Compute `s = mp.tan(mp.pi/4)` at 15 digits, then run `certify_rotation(chess(SQUARE, s), 2)` at 72 digits. It returns `None`. The stored slope is \(1-2^{-53}\), but the verifier infers a default input radius of \(10^{-56}\) from the **current** precision. The intended square at \(\tan(\pi/4)=1\) is globally period two. The same experiment with the project’s aligned regular dodecagon at \(\theta=\pi/3\) excludes its exact period-three identity. General certification requires an enclosure of the original input. The published scan independently checks that enclosure on its angular grid. [Classifier](/Users/mw/Desktop/math/remath/studies/03-sobolev-wave-attractors/experiments/phase1_probe.py:46) · [Scan enclosure checks](/Users/mw/Desktop/math/remath/studies/03-sobolev-wave-attractors/experiments/phase1_probe.py:600) · [Dodecagon mirror theorem](/Users/mw/Desktop/math/remath/studies/03-sobolev-wave-attractors/docs/phase1_regular.md:104)

**Fictitious attracting orbit.** In characteristic coordinates take the nonconvex hexagon with successive vertices \((-6,0),(0,-6),(7,1),(1,4),(0,0),(-2,4)\). The overview’s edge test accepts the rectangle \((\pm3,\pm3)\) on edges \(0,1,2,5\). All four edge fractions are strictly interior, and the formal return multiplier is \(1/2\). But the flight from \((3,3)\) toward \((-3,3)\) hits the notch at \((3/4,3)\) first. The advertised orbit does not exist. The test needs a convexity hypothesis or a first-hit check on every flight. [Claim for “any polygon”](/Users/mw/Desktop/math/remath/studies/03-sobolev-wave-attractors/docs/phase1_scouting.md:204)
