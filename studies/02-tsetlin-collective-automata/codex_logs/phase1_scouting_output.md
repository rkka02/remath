### Four theorems I would lead with

1. **Unequal memories have a unique optimal shock correlation.** For L and B, suppose \(S\le 1/2<P<R<T\), mixed-profile feedback is independent, and the memory ratio is \(\gamma>1\). The DD correlation minimizing the exponential cost of reaching cooperation is exactly \(\kappa_*=(1+1/\gamma)/2\). Under the rare path that brings the longer-memory player to its switch, this is precisely the correlation that brings the other player to its switch at the same time. In the \((.8,.6,.9,.2)\) dilemma at depths \((120,40)\), changing **only** the DD correlation from \(0\) to \(2/3\) to \(1\) gives L cooperation probabilities \(1.12438\times10^{-6}\), **\(0.517847\)**, and \(4.68957\times10^{-22}\). Their limits are \(0,1,0\). Writing \(h_x=\log(x/(1-x))\), the supremum memory ratio admitting strict cooperation is \(h_R/h_P\). [Proof and phase diagram](/Users/mw/Desktop/math/remath/studies/02-tsetlin-collective-automata/docs/phase1_optimal_correlation.md)

2. **The classical architectures have different cooperation thresholds.** In an equal-memory strict dilemma with \(p=P>1/2\), \(q=1-p\), and \(0\le\kappa<1\), the critical values of \(R\) satisfy
   \[
   F_L=F_B=\frac{p^2+\kappa pq}{p^2+q^2+2\kappa pq}
   \;<\;F_D=p+(1-\kappa)pq
   \;<\;F_K=\frac{2p}{1+p^2+\kappa pq}.
   \]
   Above its threshold a tactic selects CC. Below it, it selects DD. For the motivating game the exact correlation thresholds are **\(13/63\)** for L/B and **\(11/21\)** for Krinsky D. Krylov K defects even at perfect external correlation. The reward hypothesis matters. At \((R,P,T,S)=(.45,.20,.95,.10)\) with independent feedback, D and K select CC while the L/B mutual-cooperation probability tends to zero. 3. **An ordinary Stag Hunt can approach unanimous cooperation at finite memory and then lose it entirely.** With \((R,P,T,S)=(15/16,4/5,4/5,1/2)\), stationary mutual hunting rises from **36.04%** at depth 1 to **82.30%** at depth 16, falls to **0.43%** at depth 160, and tends to zero. An exact integer determinant already certifies cooperation above 80% at depth 10. Across this constant-hare family, the finite peak can be made arbitrarily close to one while the infinite-memory limit remains zero. The factor producing the peak is \(n\) in the stationary odds. ### What the escape calculation resolves

For \(p>1/2\), equal memories, and \(0<\kappa<1\), **the proposed diagonal exponent is exact** for the frozen corner *per excursion*. Its probability is \(\Theta(n^{-1/2}e^{-nJ})\), where \(J=\log[(p^2+\kappa pq)/(q^2+\kappa pq)]\). At independence \(J=2h_p\) and the power is zero. Allowing repeated excursions before the first action switch subtracts \(h_p\) from the exponent. Negative correlation changes the optimal route even in the square. The powers are \(0,-1/2,-3/2\) across an explicit transition. In a rectangle, the interacting game can also profit from repeated action changes by the shorter-memory player.
