**Counterexample for C3.** Take \((L,H,c,\theta)=(1/4,19/20,1/5,1/5)\) and \(\alpha=0\). Here \(p_*=91/100\), and even the depth-one share exists, with \(A_1=5/32\). Write \(s=\Pr(X\ge\theta)\). For a uniformly sampled player let \(p\) be its conditional reward probability and \(q=1-p\). Stationary depth balance gives
\[
1-2\mathbb E p
=\mathbb E[q\,\mathbf1_{\rm shallow}]-\mathbb E[p\,\mathbf1_{\rm cap}]
\le \mathbb E[q\,\mathbf1_{\rm shallow}].
\]
Stationary action balance equates the two directed switch fluxes. The common switch factor cancels for either architecture, giving \(\mathbb E[q\,\mathbf1_{\rm shallow}]=2\mathbb E[q\,\mathbf1_{\{C,\rm shallow\}}]\le2\mathbb EX\). Now \(\mathbb Ep\le L+(H-L)s\) and \(\mathbb EX\le\theta+(1-\theta)s\). Consequently
\[
s\ \ge\ \frac{1-2L-2\theta}{2(H-L+1-\theta)}
=\frac1{30}.
\]
This bound survives either order of limits.
