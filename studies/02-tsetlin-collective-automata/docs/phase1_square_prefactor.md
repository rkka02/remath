# The power of memory in the stationary odds of the motivating game

Consider linear tactics with equal depth, independent mixed-profile feedback, and independent draws over time given the current profile. Use R,P,T,S for win probabilities, q=1−P, and let κ be the DD correlation. The CC correlation can be different. The following comparison transfers a two-dimensional walk calculation to a statement about the *full stationary game*. All implicit constants can depend on the fixed game and correlations.

Suppose 1/2<P,R<1, 0<S<1/2, P≤T<1. A sufficient additional condition is that an up-step pair with independent Bernoulli marginals (S,1−T) is stochastically dominated, coordinate by coordinate as a pair, by the loss indicators at DD. With a=q²+κPq and d=P²+κPq, its four nontrivial inequalities are

$$
S\le q,\qquad 1-T\le q,\qquad S(1-T)\le a,\qquad (1-S)T\ge d .
$$

They enumerate the increasing subsets of the binary square. They hold for the motivating game (R,P,T,S)=(.8,.7,.9,.2) simultaneously for every 0≤κ≤1. Write
$$
h_R=\log\frac{R}{1-R},\qquad J=\log(d/a).
$$
Then for every **fixed** 0<κ<1,
$$
\frac{\pi_n(CC)}{\pi_n(DD)}
=\Theta\!\left(n^{-1/2}\exp[n(h_R-J)]\right).
$$
For κ=0 the power is zero, and for κ=1 it is again zero. At κ=0, J=2 log(P/q). At κ=1, J=log(P/q). At the interior critical correlation J=h_R, defection wins polynomially. The statement gives the exact power with bounded multiplicative factors. It does not identify a limiting constant for the stationary odds. For the one-coordinate marginal gambler's ruin estimate, the CC correlation can be anywhere in its feasible interval, including 1.

## From two Lindley workloads to the game

Orient signed position from deepest D, indexed 0, toward deepest C, indexed 2n−1. C starts at n. Until the first CC, a linear player's up step is a penalty in D and a reward in C. At mixed profiles the domination hypothesis and its transpose give a joint coupling below a fresh DD loss vector. At DD take the same vector. Coordinatewise reflection at zero is monotone and the finite cap can only decrease position. Thus the game, stopped on entering CC, lies below an unbounded Lindley pair W driven by i.i.d. DD loss increments (+1 on loss, −1 on win). Start both at the deep DD point z=(0,0). At a return of W to z the game has already returned there or visited CC.

Let H be the probability per excursion from z of reaching CC before the first positive return to z. An upper bound is the expected number of visits of W to [n,∞)² before its positive return, equal to the stationary joint tail divided by the invariant mass at z by the renewal reward theorem. The mass at z is positive and independent of n. For example, V(x,y)=exp(ux)+exp(uy) with small u>0 has a geometric drift outside a fixed box, and a run of joint wins empties that box. Each stationary marginal has geometric tail (q/P)^n.

For 0<κ<1 the stationary joint tail is O(n^{-1/2}e^{-Jn}). Here is a direct way to obtain the upper bound. By reversing a finite stationary-history construction and increasing its length, W has the law of the two separate running maxima of the free negative-drift walk driven by the same pairs. If X first hits n before Y, then Y must reach n after that time. Dropping the ordering constraint gives an upper bound, and exchanging labels costs a factor two. Tilt both increments by t=J/2, so the joint moment is one and the drifts of X and Y both equal 2P−1. At the free first time X reaches n, let L=Y−n. Changing measure and using the exact one-dimensional crossing probability gives the bound
$$
2e^{-Jn} E_*[e^{-tL}{\bf1}_{L\ge0}+e^{(\log(P/q)-t)L}{\bf1}_{L<0}].
$$
The overshoot L is a sum of n centered independent copies of the displacement difference between successive X upward ladder epochs under the tilt. They have exponential tails, positive variance and span two. The lattice local central limit bound gives maximal point mass O(1/√n). Both geometric series converge since 0<t<log(P/q). At independence the original joint tail is exactly (q/P)^{2n}, and under perfect synchrony it is (q/P)^n.

For the matching lower bound when 0<κ<1, use a single excursion that remains DD until the two players switch on the same step. Tilt by (t,t). Force B initial and B final simultaneous losses, with B large and fixed. Between them connect (B,B) to (n−B,n−B) in a time m=n/(2P−1)+O(√n) of the requisite parity, keeping both coordinates between 1 and n−1. The two-dimensional lattice local central limit theorem gives an unrestricted bridge mass at least c/n uniformly on a fixed-width central window in units of √n. On its first half, touching the lower sides costs at most C exp(−c'B)/n by the negative-deviation bound for a positive-drift coordinate and the O(1/n) transition bound for the remaining half. Touching the upper sides there is exponentially unlikely in n. Reverse the last half to obtain the other two bounds. Choose B once to keep a positive fraction of the bridge mass. There are order √n disjoint first-arrival times. The likelihood ratio is precisely e^{-Jn}, giving H≥c n^{-1/2}e^{-Jn}.

For κ=0 use reversibility of the two independent frozen reflected coordinates. Their invariant point mass at (n,n) is (1−q/P)²(q/P)^{2n}. Run backward from this point. With uniformly positive probability both coordinates move below n on the first step and stay below n until they jointly reach z. To see this, take B initial simultaneous steps toward z. Before each coordinate first reaches zero, its probability of returning to level n is at most (q/P)^B. During the next Cn steps, returns from zero to level n have probability O(n(q/P)^n). Choose large C so both coordinates are within a fixed distance of zero at Cn with probability arbitrarily near one, and finish with a fixed run of wins. In a stationary cycle the first visit to either threshold can occur only once. Time reversal and the cycle formula therefore give H≥c(q/P)^{2n}. For κ=1 the frozen attempt is one gambler's ruin excursion. These arguments obtain both bounds on H.

## Renewal and mixing

Each CC spell has expected length of order exp(n h_R), uniformly over its entrance state. For the upper bound follow one coordinate to its first switch. From deepest C the mean is given by a geometric sum of order exp(n h_R). For the lower bound, from any entrance a fixed run of joint rewards puts both a fixed distance B inside C. A gambler's ruin bound in each coordinate, followed by concentration over O(n) steps and a fixed run of joint rewards near the deep end, gives a probability bounded below of reaching the deep CC point without leaving. From deep CC the probability to exit by time m is O(m exp(−n h_R)) by marginal domination by stationary reflected coordinates and the union bound. A single-switch exit followed by a successful mixed transition has the same exponential scale when it is possible. At perfectly synchronous CC the first exit from deep CC flips both players.

Each spell outside CC has expected length of order H^{-1}. Starting from any outside state, the time to hit z or CC is O(n) in mean. For this assertion start the dominating W at (2n,2n). The exponential drift gives E V(W_{Cn})≤C' with C fixed large. With uniformly positive probability the pair is then in a fixed box and a fixed run of joint wins empties it. Repeat in O(n)-step blocks from the finite game. From an actual CC exit there is, uniformly, a positive probability of hitting z before CC. Protect a newly switched D coordinate from switching for Cn steps. Its win probability in D is at least P, so the probability of protection is bounded below by the one-dimensional gambler's ruin estimate (after reaching deep D, the probability of losing protection within Cn is exponentially small). At the block end the dominating W is outside a sufficiently large fixed box with probability smaller than half that lower bound. A fixed run of joint wins then reaches z. From z the number of trials to reach CC is geometric with parameter H. Each trial has length at least one and mean at most the fixed mean return time of W to z. It spends its initial step in DD. Thus the expected occupation of DD in an outside spell is also of order H^{-1}.

Mixed occupation divided by DD occupation tends to zero. Per trial from z its expectation is bounded by the mean occupation of the event max(W_i)≥n in a W cycle, which is O((q/P)^n). Before the first trial there are O(n) outside steps in mean. Applying renewal reward at successive entries to CC (or the finite stationary entry law) proves the odds formula and the assertion about critical selection.

For fixed 0<ε<1/2, including exponential ties, the worst-initial-state total-variation mixing time has order
$$
t_{\rm mix}(\epsilon)=\Theta\big(\min\{H^{-1},e^{nh_R}\}\big).
$$
From deep DD, the probability of reaching CC by time m is at most mH, since at most m trials can start. From deep CC the probability of leaving CC by that time is O(m exp(−n h_R)). If the stationary CC mass is at least one half, start in deep DD and test for CC. Otherwise start in deep CC and test the same event. At a sufficiently small multiple of the displayed scale this gives total variation greater than ε.

For the upper bound, choose the deep corner whose escape scale is the larger of the two and denote that scale by L. From every state it is reached with probability at least 1/2 by time T, a sufficiently large constant times the smaller scale. During CC the time to either leave or reach its deep corner has mean O(n): in a block of Cn steps the frozen reflected marginal drift puts both deficits in a fixed box with fixed positive probability, where joint rewards can empty it. Repeating blocks handles arbitrary starting states. Combined with the spell estimates and the uniformly positive deepening probabilities, this bounds the mean time to the selected deep point from every state. Markov's inequality gives the hit by T. From that corner the probability of occupying the corner again at any specified time up to ηL is bounded below by a constant for sufficiently small fixed η. For CC, use the homogeneous reflected CC walk started at zero and subtract the probability of an exit. For DD, use the dominating W and subtract the probability of a successful trial. Each reflected walk started at zero is coordinatewise below its stationary counterpart, giving a positive time-uniform floor for its origin mass. Since T/L is bounded, split any residual time up to T into a bounded number of intervals of length at most ηL and multiply the return lower bounds by the Markov property. This works even when the escape scales are comparable. At time T there is thus a state-independent positive lower bound on the mass at the chosen corner. Iterating this minorization proves the mixing upper bound.

In the motivating example at independence the **time to hit CC from deep DD** grows on the scale (7/3)^{2n}, whereas **mixing** grows on the scale 4^n. Across the defection phase, increasing the DD correlation decreases the passage exponent while leaving the mixing exponent log 4 fixed. In the cooperation phase the worst initial condition lies in DD, and the two scales agree.
